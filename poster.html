Seeing the Invisible

Physics-Guided Missing-Physics Learning for PEMFC Flooding Diagnosis

Gang-hyeon Shin1, Jong-jin Jang1, Geon-ho Shin1, Jae-hyeon Ryu, Seo-yeon Kim, Min-seo Baek, Min-ji Lee, Chan-seo Lee
Advisor: Prof. Kee-Youn Yo

1Department of Chemical Engineering, Seoul National University of Science and Technology

Abstract

Liquid water accumulation in porous gas diffusion layers critically limits mass transport in polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells, yet the internal flooding state is difficult to observe directly. More broadly, physics-based multiphysics models often rely on empirical or fitted constitutive relations to represent unresolved transport behavior, making constitutive uncertainty a major source of prediction error. Here, we propose a physics-informed grey-box framework that retains the known governing equations within a finite-element solver while learning only the missing constitutive physics. A learnable gas-phase transport closure is embedded directly into the two-phase FEM model and identified from synthetic reference responses using discrete-adjoint sensitivities, without prescribing a fixed functional form in advance. After training at RH 50%, the learned constitutive law is frozen and evaluated at unseen RH 70% on two discretizations, 16 × 40 and 32 × 80. Agreement with the synthetic reference is retained without retraining in the reported test cases, supporting transfer of the learned closure across the tested humidity and discretization changes. These synthetic tests motivate future experimental validation for flooding diagnosis and physics-guided digital twins.

Conclusions

  1. Physics-preserving learning. Governing equations remain in FEM; only the uncertain gas-phase closure gθ(φg) is learned.
  2. Constitutive recovery. 2.16% relative L² error at epoch 179 over the evaluated support.
  3. Frozen transfer. Agreement at RH 70% on both 16 × 40 and 32 × 80, without retraining.

Saturation-field errors: 0.55% coarse · 0.49% fine.

Synthetic verification only. Experimental validation and wider operating-domain tests remain future work.
